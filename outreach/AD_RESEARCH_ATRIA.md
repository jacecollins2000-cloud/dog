# Ad research session 1: Atria (Sep 28, 2026)

**Result:** 18 references, 12 customer concerns to verify, 7 landing pages compared (mobile screenshots in `outreach/ad-research/`), 3 finished briefs.
Every item below records *why* it was kept. Longevity and saves are clues that an ad is worth studying, not proof it's profitable. What an advertiser claims isn't proof either.

Builds on `VSL_PLAN.md` (Meta Ad Library pull earlier today). New since then: **Pixis** now sells a see-your-homepage-first offer, **Owner.com** launched an *instant AI* redesign preview, and **GoDaddy** is running Stand Out's own positioning line ("your website should be unique").

## How the session ran

Settings: English → active only → longest running, plus "most saved". Atria doesn't count shares, so its closest measure is **most saved**: how many Atria workspaces saved an ad.

What Atria could and couldn't do:

- **Keyword search is loose.** "website design" sorted by longest-running returned pond builders, pool builders and carpet installers (it matches "design" alone). Sorting by relevance with a *minimum days running* filter worked much better, so that's how "longest running" was applied.
- **"Most saved" ignores the keyword** in a library-wide search (it returned supplement ads). It works per advertiser, so it was run on each web-design advertiser found.
- **The Atria plan's API allowance ran out partway (HTTP 402).** Not done because of that: saving to an Atria board, four more transcripts, the Atria note. The ad IDs are listed at the bottom so they can be saved later. Landing pages were studied outside Atria.
- Not in Atria: The Free Website Guys. Topline Pro: no active ads (their last ones ran 0–33 days).

### Framework notes, one per reference

**R1. StoneSystems: "$297/month. Website + A.I"** (transcript read)
- **Desire:** Steady jobs without door-knocking or waiting on referrals.
- **Avatar:** US contractor who's been pitched "X jobs in X days" and still follows up leads by hand.
- **Awareness:** Knows agencies and is sick of them. The hook assumes you've heard the pitch.
- **Sophistication:** Mocks the saturated promise ("Contractors, I'll get you 30 jobs in the next 30 days or you don't pay me. Like, aren't you sick of freaking hearing marketing agencies say the same exact thing?"), then gives the price at second 12 ("I'll just literally tell you our price, like straight up front") and even admits the upsell.
- **Angle:** The agency that tells you the price and the catch.
- **Execution:** Founder to camera, fast and casual; deliverables in order (rebuild site, text follow-up, reviews, missed-call AI, Google profile); closes with "if you … don't want to get scammed by more marketing agencies".
- **For Stand Out:** Say $399 early and say what happens if they don't like it. → Brief 1.

**R2. StoneSystems: "Home Service Contractors"** (354 days, transcript read)
- **Desire:** Marketing that works.
- **Avatar:** Contractor who spent on Google, SEO and Facebook ads and says "my marketing doesn't work".
- **Awareness:** Problem-aware and has already tried to fix it.
- **Sophistication:** Reframes the failure: ads aren't broken, the basics are missing. "Once you have the basic systems in place and you actually look good online, that's when … paid ads will actually start to convert."
- **Angle:** Fix the foundation before you spend on ads.
- **Execution:** Talking head, teaching tone, almost no pitch.
- **For Stand Out:** A diagnostic ad: "Before you pay for ads, look at the page they land on." Supports concern #1.

**R3. StoneSystems: the "No door knocking…" copy** (about 20 variants, 318–354 days)
- **Desire:** Work coming in without hustling for it.
- **Avatar:** Owner living on referrals and hustle.
- **Awareness:** Problem-aware.
- **Sophistication:** Lists the tiring alternatives ("No door knocking… No relying on word-of-mouth… No crossing fingers"), then "our Gift to you, FREE Website ALL Done-For-You… but that's just the beginning".
- **Angle:** A free site as the way into their system.
- **Execution:** Short lines, ellipses, emoji bullets. The copy stays the same while the video changes.
- **For Stand Out:** A "No ___" opener ("No sales call. No monthly fee. No template."). Keep the copy fixed and test the video.

**R4. Blue Collar Marketing: "Web & Logo Design for Contractors"** (686 days, transcript read)
- **Desire:** More high-paying jobs, from an agency that respects the trade.
- **Avatar:** Contractor who "wasted time and money on agencies that just don't get it".
- **Awareness:** Solution-aware and burned.
- **Sophistication:** Identity. It opens "Here's Blue Collar Marketing, and here's everyone else", then three numbers: 500 trade companies, 35 states, "number one".
- **Angle:** The agency that's one of you.
- **Execution:** Split-screen comparison, "I'll make this quick because I know you're busy", a crude job-site skit (the apprentice who won't show up sober), ending "No bullshit, just growth."
- **For Stand Out:** Borrow the split-screen opening and the "I know you're busy" respect. Skip the numbers; Stand Out can't back them yet. → Brief 3.

**R5. Blue Collar Marketing: "No Fluff. Just Results."** (their most-saved)

## Step 6: Three creative briefs

Rules for all three: the seven sites are labelled **concept sites** on screen; no testimonials or lead numbers until real clients exist. The offer matches the live page: **48-hour homepage preview, $100 deposit that counts toward the price and is refundable if they don't love the preview, from $399 one time, live in 7 days, no sales call.** Format: 9:16 video, captions burned in, koi logo end card (`outreach/videos/logo-koi.mp4`).

### Brief 1: "Price before you ask"

- **References:** R1 (StoneSystems, most saved and #1 by impressions), R10 (Owner.com's enemy with a number), R15 (48 hours + refund in the headline).
- **Avatar:** A Vegas/Henderson trade owner who gets agency calls and DMs weekly and assumes every pitch hides a monthly fee.
- **Desire:** A good website without being sold to.
- **Awareness:** Has heard "more leads, guaranteed" many times.
- **Sophistication:** High. Promises about leads are worn out, so answer with things they can check: the price, the days, the refund terms.
- **Angle:** Everything up front: the price, the timeline, and what happens if you don't like it.
- **Opening (0–3s):** Jace to camera, phone in hand. On-screen text: **"$399. One time."** Voiceover: *"I'm going to tell you my price before you have to ask."*
- **Body:**
  - 3–10s: *"Most web design ads make you book a call to find out. Here's the whole deal."*
  - 10–25s: A concept site scrolling at phone size, labelled "Concept site". *"Send me your business name and a few photos. In 48 hours you'll see your new homepage."*
  - 25–40s: Text cards: "$100 deposit → counts toward the $399" · "Don't love the preview? You get it back." · "Live in 7 days. No monthly fee."
  - 40–50s: *"The other option is $99 a month. That's $1,188 a year, every year."*
  - CTA: *"Tap the button. Two-minute form. I'll start yours today."*
- **Primary text:**
  > Price first, so you don't have to ask: $399, one time.
  > Send your business name and a few photos. In 48 hours you'll see your new homepage.
  > $100 deposit to start. It counts toward the price, and you get it back if you don't love the preview.
  > No monthly fee. No sales call.
- **Headline:** $399 one time. See it in 48 hours.
- **Static version:** A plain text card, "$399. One time. Your homepage preview in 48 hours. Refundable deposit.", with a phone mockup of a concept site.
- **Reason recorded:** StoneSystems' ad that leads with its price and mocks agency hype is both its most-saved ad and its #1 of 159 by impressions after 146 days. Owner.com's longest runners (175 days) name a fee as the enemy. Stand Out's own call sheet logs a lead who objected to paying upfront, so the refund has to be said plainly.
- **Don't copy:** StoneSystems' "30 jobs in 30 days" parody line. The idea to borrow is transparency, not the wording.

### Brief 2: "See it before you pay"

- **References:** R7 (Pixis, same offer but call-gated), R14 (Fido & Frame's skeptic structure), R13 (redesign reveal), R8 (Owner.com's instant AI version).
- **Avatar:** An owner whose site is dated or dead (the call sheet has several dead domains and a GoDaddy page untouched since 2022) and who assumes a redesign means months of meetings.
- **Desire:** A site they're proud to send people to, without taking on a project.
- **Awareness:** Knows the site is bad; hasn't looked for a fix.
- **Sophistication:** Medium and rising. Pixis and Owner.com now both offer a look first. Stand Out's answer: no call, and a preview built around one idea for their business.
- **Angle:** Your actual homepage, in your inbox in 48 hours, before you commit.
- **Opening (0–3s):** Split screen. Left: a dated site from `/before/`. Right: its concept site loading. Text: **"Before → 48 hours later."** Voiceover: *"This is what 48 hours looks like."*
- **Body:**
  - 3–15s: Screen recording, Framer style: scroll the old site, wipe to the concept, and show its one idea (the glass shop's cracked glass healing).
  - 15–30s: Jace: *"I'll be honest, most people think a new website means three months of meetings. Mine starts with your business name. No call."*
  - 30–40s: Three-step card: "1. Send your name + photos → 2. See your homepage in 48 hours → 3. Love it? Live in 7 days."
  - 40–50s: Offer card: "$100 deposit, refundable. Counts toward $399."
